Leaf-level model

Parameters and forcing

PowderyMildew.MildewParameters — Type
MildewParameters(; b_c, b_a, b_ext = 0.0, tau_s = Inf, K = 1, epsilon = 0.0, latent = 0.0,
                 L = 0, gamma_I = 0.0, c_w = 1.0)

Parameters of the leaf-level model:

  • b_c: secondary (conidial) transmission per day per unit sporulating leaf fraction;
  • b_a: infections per unit of released primary inoculum (ascospores);
  • b_ext: background force of infection from outside the stand, per day;
  • tau_s: mean time (days) a young leaf stays fully susceptible, Erlang with K stages (Inf for leaves that never mature);
  • epsilon: susceptibility of mature leaves relative to young ones;
  • latent: mean latent period (days) at unit development rate (see development in SeasonForcing), Erlang with L stages; L = 0 makes infected leaves sporulate at once;
  • gamma_I: rate (per day) at which sporulation stops;
  • c_w: next year's primary inoculum per unit of gated sporulating leaf-days.

PowderyMildew.SeasonForcing — Type
SeasonForcing(; tspan, leaves0 = 0.0, production = t -> 0.0, release = t -> 0.0, pulse = false,
              weather = t -> 1.0, development = t -> 1.0, gate = nothing, inoculations = [],
              tstops = Float64[])

The forcing of one season, over tspan = (t0, t1) in days (day of year, say):

  • leaves0: young susceptible leaves present at t0;
  • production: leaf production l(t), e.g. a FlushProduction;
  • release: the hazard g(t) of primary-inoculum release, e.g. a ReleaseHazard; pulse = true instead releases all of it at t0, infecting a share 1 - exp(-b_a C) of the leaves present (the fast-release limit);
  • weather: the multiplier f(t) of the force of infection, e.g. beta_response of a daily temperature series;
  • development: d(t) ≥ 0, the rate of latent development relative to the one at which the mean latent period is latent, e.g. degree_day_rate of a daily temperature series; the latent stages then advance at (L/latent) d(t), so the latent period is shorter in warm weather;
  • gate: η(t), the weight of sporulating leaves in next year's inoculum; nothing uses the sporulating leaves at the end of the season, as Hamelin et al. (2016) do;
  • inoculations: added inoculum, as (day, dose) pairs with t0 < day < t1. At each, a share 1 - exp(-dose f(day)) of the young susceptible leaves (1 - exp(-ε dose f(day)) of the mature ones) is infected at once, as when spores are blown onto seedlings;
  • tstops: times the solver must step to, such as day boundaries of daily forcings.

PowderyMildew.FlushProduction — Type
FlushProduction(dates, spreads, sizes)

Leaf production l(t) from flushes: flush f puts out sizes[f] leaves (as a fraction of the stand's maximum) with normally distributed emergence dates, mean dates[f] and standard deviation spreads[f] (days), so l(t) = Σ_f sizes[f] φ((t - dates[f]) / spreads[f]) / spreads[f]. The spread stands for differences in flushing between trees.

source
PowderyMildew.ReleaseHazard — Type
ReleaseHazard(; rate, optimum, curvature, intercept = 0.0, wet = t -> 1.0)

The release hazard of primary inoculum, g(t) = rate ψ(t) wet(t), with the readiness of chasmothecia ψ(t) = logistic(intercept + curvature (t - optimum)²), the logit-quadratic in day of year fitted by Marçais, Kavkova & Desprez-Loustau (2009); curvature < 0. wet(t) (between 0 and 1) gates release by rain or free moisture, for instance a DailySeries.

source
PowderyMildew.DailySeries — Type
DailySeries(values; first_day = 1)

A piecewise-constant function of time from daily values: day first_day + k - 1 (the interval [first_day + k - 1, first_day + k)) takes values[k]. Use it for daily weather.

source
PowderyMildew.beta_response — Function
beta_response(T; Tmin = 5.0, Topt = 21.5, Tmax = 30.0)

A temperature response between 0 and 1: zero outside (Tmin, Tmax) and 1 at Topt, the beta function of Yin et al. (1995), ((T - Tmin)/(Topt - Tmin)) ((Tmax - T)/(Tmax - Topt))^((Tmax - Topt)/(Topt - Tmin)). The defaults follow the germination of E. alphitoides conidia reported by Marçais & Desprez-Loustau (2014): high at 15–25 °C, optimum 20–23 °C, none at 30 °C.

source
PowderyMildew.FungicideSchedule — Type
FungicideSchedule(dates; efficacy, decay)

Protection by a fungicide applied on dates (days): a multiplier of the force of infection, 1 - efficacy * exp(-(t - t_last) / decay) with t_last the latest application on or before t, and 1 before the first. efficacy (between 0 and 1) is the reduction just after an application and decay (days) the e-folding time of its residual activity. Compose it with the weather, e.g. weather = t -> beta_response(T(t)) * schedule(t), and put the dates in tstops.

source
PowderyMildew.degree_day_rate — Function
degree_day_rate(T; base, reference)

A linear thermal-time development rate, max(T - base, 0) / (reference - base): 0 at or below base and 1 at reference (°C). Used as development in SeasonForcing, it makes the latent period last latent days at reference and a fixed number of degree-days above base.

Problem and solution

PowderyMildew.MildewProblem — Type
MildewProblem(p::MildewParameters, forcings, C0)

The leaf-level model run over consecutive seasons, one SeasonForcing each, starting with primary inoculum C0. Leaves are renewed every season; the inoculum carries over through the winter map C = c_w W (see MildewParameters). Solve it with solve.

source
CommonSolve.solve — Function
solve(prob::HamelinProblem; alg = Tsit5(), abstol = 1e-12, reltol = 1e-10)

Integrate each season of prob with the ODE solver alg and apply between_season between seasons. Returns a HamelinSolution.

source
solve(prob::MildewProblem; alg = Tsit5(), abstol = 1e-12, reltol = 1e-10)

Integrate each season of prob and apply the winter map between seasons. Returns a MildewSolution.

source
solve(prob::SpatialMildewProblem; alg = Tsit5(), abstol = 1e-12, reltol = 1e-10)

Integrate each season of prob over all stands at once, with the airborne field solved at every right-hand-side evaluation from one sparse Cholesky factorisation, and apply each stand's winter map between seasons. Returns a SpatialMildewSolution.

source
PowderyMildew.MildewSolution — Type
MildewSolution

The solution of a MildewProblem: one dense ODE solution per season. sol(season, t) is the state on day t of season, laid out as [U₁ … U_K; M; E₁ … E_L; I; Z; C; W]. See leaf_area, incidence, visible_incidence and winter_inoculum.

source
PowderyMildew.leaf_area — Function
leaf_area(sol::MildewSolution, season, t)

The leaves present on day t of season (young, mature, latent, sporulating and spent), as a fraction of the stand's maximum.

visible_incidence(sol::MildewSolution, season, t; latent_visible = 0.0)

The share of leaves present on day t of season with visible mildew: sporulating and spent leaves, plus a share latent_visible of latent ones.
