PowderyMildew

Seasonal, leaf-level models of oak powdery mildew (Erysiphe alphitoides and relatives on Quercus robur and Q. petraea). It holds a faithful implementation of the seasonal model of Hamelin et al. (2016, doi:10.1002/ecs2.1517), the baseline against which the package's extensions (leaf flushes and ageing, a latent stage, weather forcing, a chasmothecial winter reservoir and spatial inoculum) are checked.

source

PowderyMildew.jl models oak powdery mildew (Erysiphe alphitoides and relatives on Quercus robur and Q. petraea): when epidemics start, how severe they become, and how they spread. It has three layers, each tested against the one below.

  1. Hamelin et al. (2016), Ecosphere 7:e01517. The seasonal two-species model, exactly (HamelinProblem), with its annual map, the binomial likelihood of species frequencies, and the coexistence closed form for the between-season coefficients (coexistence_chi), which reproduces the paper's Table 2.
  2. The leaf-level model (MildewProblem). Leaf flushes, Erlang leaf ageing and latency, secondary (conidial) and primary (ascospore) infection with a timed release, weather forcing, and a winter map through chasmothecia. It nests the one-species Hamelin model exactly.
  3. The spatial model (SpatialMildewProblem). The leaf-level model on every cell of a grid of oak stands, coupled by two-scale dispersal: a share 1 − ρ of conidia infects within the stand and a share ρ escapes into a quasi-steady screened-Poisson field with length scale ℓ. A uniform landscape reduces to the leaf-level model.

Tutorials

  • The model of Hamelin et al. (2016): the model, Table 2, and why its between-season coefficients only restate the assumed initial incidence.
  • Timing and severity: the leaf-level model, its nesting of Hamelin's, and how the timing of flushing against ascospore release sets severity.
  • Spread: the dispersal field, front speeds, and spread across a patchy landscape.

The tutorials use illustrative parameter values; the package has not yet been fitted to data.

Quick start

using PowderyMildew

p = MildewParameters(; b_c = 0.25, b_a = 0.5, tau_s = 20, K = 3, epsilon = 0.05, latent = 8, L = 3,
                     gamma_I = 0.05, c_w = 2.0)
season = SeasonForcing(; tspan = (90, 280), production = FlushProduction([120, 190], [5, 8], [0.8, 0.2]),
                       release = ReleaseHazard(; rate = 0.2, optimum = 140, curvature = -0.003),
                       gate = t -> t > 240 ? 1.0 : 0.0, tstops = [240.0])
sol = solve(MildewProblem(p, fill(season, 5), 1.0))
visible_incidence(sol, 5, 280.0)

land = Landscape(ones(100, 1); cellsize = 1.0)
spread = solve(SpatialMildewProblem(p, land, Dispersal(; rho = 0.2, ell = 4.0), fill(season, 5),
                                    [1.0; zeros(99)]))
visible_incidence(spread, 5, 280.0)    # one value per stand
